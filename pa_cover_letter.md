Dear Editor,

I am submitting the enclosed Letter, "An error budget for political-bias
measurement in large language models," for consideration at *Political
Analysis*.

A large and growing literature reports that large language models lean
left of centre on standardized political instruments. This Letter shows
that the measurement apparatus used to obtain that finding, not only the
models themselves, accounts for most of the variance in a reported score.
Administering the Political Compass and 8Values to 19 models 60 times each,
I decompose a measured political position into variance from model
identity, instrument choice, and trial noise, finding that instrument
choice alone moves a score roughly four times more than which model is
audited. Crossing two further instruments into the same model collapses
the model-identity share further, to under 2%, and three additional
measurement factors examined in their own factorial designs, asker
identity, instrument contamination, and response format, each explain more
variance than model identity does under the field's standard protocol.

This paper is a measurement-validity contribution to a literature that has,
so far, quantified each of these apparatus effects only in isolation. It
engages directly with the closest prior work on this question, including a
recent three-instrument, 26-model study, and proposes a minimum reporting
standard that would make the existing literature's estimates comparable to
one another for the first time.

The manuscript is a Letter (approximately 1,150 words, one figure and one
table), with a web appendix containing full methods, the complete
four-instrument crossing, and supporting robustness checks. All data and
code are released publicly and will be included on the title page and
restored to the appendix upon acceptance, after anonymized review.

This manuscript is not under review elsewhere and has not been previously
published. I have no competing interests to declare.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Sachin Letchumanan
