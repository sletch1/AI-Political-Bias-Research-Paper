Dear Editor,

I am submitting the enclosed Research Article, "An error budget for
political-bias measurement in large language models," for consideration
at *Political Analysis*.

A large and growing literature reports that large language models lean
left of centre on standardized political instruments. This article shows
that the measurement apparatus used to obtain that finding, not only the
models themselves, accounts for most of the variance in a reported score.
Administering the Political Compass and 8Values to 19 models 60 times
each, I decompose a measured political position into variance from model
identity, instrument choice, and trial noise, finding that instrument
choice alone moves a score roughly four times more than which model is
audited. Crossing two further instruments into the same model collapses
the model-identity share further, to under 2%, and three additional
measurement factors examined in their own factorial designs, asker
identity, instrument contamination, and response format, each explain
more variance than model identity does under the field's standard
protocol.

This paper is a measurement-validity contribution to a literature that
has, so far, quantified each of these apparatus effects only in
isolation. It engages directly with the closest prior work on this
question, including a recent three-instrument, 26-model study, and two
recent Political Analysis papers on the reliability of LLM outputs used
as measurements, and proposes a minimum reporting standard that would
make the existing literature's estimates comparable to one another for
the first time.

The manuscript is a Research Article (approximately 5,800 words), with a
web appendix, submitted as supplementary material, containing full
methods, the complete four-instrument crossing, and supporting robustness
checks. All data and code are released publicly and are cited in the
manuscript's Data Availability Statement.

Portions of this manuscript's text were drafted and revised with the
assistance of a commercial AI writing assistant, used interactively
throughout the project's development in 2026 and accessed via a paid
subscription; portions of the underlying data-collection pipeline and
analysis code were developed the same way. I directed all content and
analysis decisions, independently verified every reported number against
the underlying data, and take full responsibility for the manuscript's
content and claims. A full description of this use is in the manuscript's
Acknowledgements section and in the appendix's Methods.

This manuscript is not under review elsewhere and has not been previously
published. I have no competing interests to declare.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Sachin Letchumanan
Virginia Tech
