Dear Editors,

I am submitting "An error budget for political-bias measurement in large language models" as a Research Article to
*Research & Politics*.

Why the article suits the journal. Large language models are now a source of political information, and a growing
literature audits their political lean by administering questionnaires such as the Political Compass. The article
asks a question that political scientists already know how to ask of any measure: how much of a score is the object
measured, and how much is the instrument? Using generalizability theory and the multitrait-multimethod matrix, it
shows that on 19 models administered four instruments, the part of a model's position that transfers across
instruments is small: an estimated 17% (95% CI [0, 40]) on the two original instruments and 2% ([0, 18]) on four,
while between half and two-thirds of a score depends on the instrument and axis and a third is trial noise. The two
original instruments converge on the social construct (r = 0.51) but not on the economic one (r = 0.005), so the mean
convergent correlation (0.26, SE 0.17) does not exceed the correlation between different constructs on one instrument
(0.52). A positive control shows that the instruments can agree: each model's shift between a progressive and a
conservative asker correlates at r = 0.86 across them. Differences between models in a published "lean" are therefore
largely not comparable across studies. The article offers a minimum reporting standard for researchers who use language
models as measurement instruments. It is short (about 3,700 words including references; one figure, two tables) and
comes with an online appendix, and all data, scoring code and analysis scripts reproduce every number offline from the
archived raw trials. I state plainly what the data do not show, including that the transferable share is imprecisely
estimated and that instrument and axis effects are not cleanly separable.

Declarations. The manuscript is not under review elsewhere and has not been published; a preprint may be posted and its
DOI entered at submission. I have no competing interests. Writing and coding assistance from a commercial AI tool is
acknowledged on the title page and described here as required: it was used interactively for portions of the prose and
for the analysis code, it generated no data, and I reviewed all of its output and take full responsibility. The
manuscript is double-anonymized; identifying details are on the title page only. The research involved no human
participants. I understand that publication carries an article processing charge payable on acceptance.
